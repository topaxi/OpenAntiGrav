# Wipeout HD's video decodes, and the logo reel is the Studio Liverpool ident

2026-08-17, the sibling of the soundtrack and built the same way: read the container, hand the codec to what already decodes codecs. **HD's video is RAD's Bink**, 37 files and 101.2 MiB - two 1080p studio logo reels, 16 circuit previews at 352x256, 19 front-end mode icons - and `oag_formats::bik` reads the header on all 37 with three arithmetic invariants each. **The finding worth carrying is that a `.bik` is little-endian**, alone among HD's payloads: everything else on the disc is Pulse's format byte-swapped, and this one is not, because the container is the PC authoring tool's rather than the console's. Anyone porting a reader to HD by adding a `ByteOrder` argument will reach for one here and get `0x80070000` pixels wide. **[ADR-0024](../docs/architecture/adr/0024-in-process-codecs-and-ffmpeg-as-a-last-resort.md)'s category check was made rather than inherited**, which is the thing that ADR asks for and the reason it exists: category 1 has exactly one candidate, `infinitier_bik_decoder` 0.0.14, and it is **GPL-3.0-or-later**, which the licence bar excludes - `nihav` has Bink decoders and is not on crates.io at all; category 2 is closed by the rule, Bink being RAD's format and not Wipeout's. So category 3, and the transcode is nine lines around the cache that was already there. The **container header** is still read here, as `.PMF`'s and `.IPF`'s are, and that is what buys the degradation a program stream cannot offer: with no `ffmpeg` at all a `.bik` still reports a correct frame count, size and rate. **Cost, measured before wiring anything**: the 531-frame 1080p reel is ~98 s of `libaom` and 21 MiB of cache, once - same order as the PSP intro at a twenty-sixth of the pixels. **What decoding it settled**: `oag_hd::frontend::names::STUDIO_LOGO_MOVIE`'s doc comment recorded that the name was the whole of the evidence for what the file held; it is the Studio Liverpool ident, 8.86 s resolving out of black to a `STUDIO Liverpool` card. What it did *not* settle is which of the two reels a PS3 plays - that is still the which-`skin.xml` question. `crates/game/tests/hd_movie_ground_truth.rs` (3 tests, all disc-backed), [bik.md](../docs/formats/bik.md). **One thing left open, and the second item this row used to carry is closed.** **The audio is measured and not played.** Six of the 37 files carry tracks - the two reels four each, 48 kHz stereo DCT - and every one of those numbers is asserted against the disc, but Bink's audio lives *inside* the video container where ATRAC3+ sits beside it, so playing it needs a second `ffmpeg` route and a widened `Movie::audio`, a change to a shared type and to `boot::load_movie_sound`. It was deferred on the grounds that **nothing would consume it** - and that reason expired within the session, because the front end got wired and `Studio Logo` now plays the reel on screen. So the remaining blocker is the shape of `Movie::audio` alone, the boot prints `audio: Studio Logo plays silently` where it would name a track, and ADR-0019's consequence now has a real consumer to be got wrong for. **What is no longer open**: the app draws a `.bik` - `just play hd` plays the reel through HD's own chain, and `--movie <path>` reaches any of the 37.
